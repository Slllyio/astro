# Why only 273 of 396

Measured 2026-10-24 on the confirmed golden pool after the chart-fidelity re-base
(273/396 exact = 68.9%, 363/396 within one step = 91.7%, 33 inversions). Every number
below comes from a dump of all 396 rows with the pillars, fired rules and judge clause that
produced each verdict, plus a text-mining pass over the Raman passages each row cites.
Nothing in the engine was changed to produce this document.

## The verdict in one paragraph

The engine is not wrong about Raman most of the time. It is wrong about the SHAPE of his
answer. 90 of the 123 misses have the label "mixed" on one side: Raman wrote a two-sided
conclusion and the engine picked a pole, or the engine returned a stalemate where Raman
decided. Under that sits a deeper defect: the two "strength" pillars the judge leans on
(Shadbala `is_powerful` for the house lord and the karaka) say "strong" for almost every
planet in almost every chart, so they carry no information, while Raman's own word
"weak" in the same passages predicts his verdict well. A third, smaller cause is a handful of
rules whose polarity is right for the sentence they cite but wrong for the matter they are
scored against. A fourth is plain coverage: on 21 rows no rule fired at all.

are not the problem.

## 2. Cause A: the "mixed" tier is two different things (90 of 123 misses)

**A1. Raman's mixed is a two-sided conclusion, usually in sequence.** Of the 59 rows where
Raman is labelled mixed, the engine misses 40. Reading them: two marriages, the second after
the first wife's death (HTJAH-II h7_07, NH chart_37); emperor, then abdication (Napoleon,
HTJAH-II h10_05); three elder co-borns living, one dead (h11_03); school education poor but
self-taught to a high post (h6_12, x80); robust constitution, then ruined health (NH chart_26).
The engine has no concept of "both poles operative" or "good, then lost in period X". It
weighs testimony to a single pole and the nearest it can say is a stalemate.

**A2. The engine's mixed is mostly a non-answer.** Of the 69 rows where the engine says mixed,
Raman is decisive in 50. The sources: clause 2 on stated-position charts (26 rows), clause 2
on cast charts (24), clause 8 borderline (16). On the stated-position rows Raman's reasons are
plain and single-poled ("These are very powerful combinations endowing the native with
immense wealth", NH chart_18; "The position of Rahu in the 9th indicates early death to his
father", NH chart_19). The engine had the rules that fire on those very facts; it had no
pillar to break the tie and so declined.

A small part of A1 is label quality, not engine: a few mixed labels read as weak-afflicted
or weak-favourable rather than two-sided (HTJAH-I chart_12 H1, HTJAH-II h11_03 H11). That is
worth a precise definition of "mixed" in the fixture (see R3) but it is a handful of rows, not
the 40.

## 3. Cause B: the strength pillars do not discriminate (the deepest defect)

On the 292 cast charts, `lord_strong` is True in 260 (89%) and `karaka_strong` in 242
(83%); both strong and the lord not hard-afflicted in 190 (65%). Conditional on
`lord_strong` True, Raman's verdict is favourable 119, afflicted 100, mixed 41: a coin flip.
This is the same shape that led to retiring the Bhava Bala pillar (97.5% "strong").

Text-mining the cited passages for Raman's own words about the lord and karaka
(254 rows name the lord's condition, 269 the karaka's):

| Raman says the lord is | Engine lord_strong True | Engine lord_strong False |
|---|---|---|
| strong / well placed / unafflicted | 115 | 15 |
| weak / afflicted / debilitated / badly placed | **51** | 13 |

When Raman calls the lord weak, the engine calls it strong four times out of five. The karaka
is the same (48 of 62). Raman's word, by contrast, predicts his own verdict: lord "weak" →
afflicted 53 of 77 (69%); lord "strong" → favourable 88 of 149 (59%). What he means by
"weak" is visible in every example: association with a malefic, aspect by a malefic,
placement in the 6th/8th/12th from the house or in the 12th from the matter, uncancelled
debility, combustion. Never a Shadbala figure.

Why the 2026-10-24 disposition pre-registration did not catch this: variant V1 used the
influence axis only when it read "bad", and let "mixed" fall back to Shadbala. But Raman's
"weak" lords read "mixed" on that axis 38 times out of 64 and "bad" only 5 times, because any
benefic contact flips the reading to mixed, while Raman still calls the lord afflicted when
the malefic contact dominates. The axis measured was not the thing Raman uses, so its null
result does not close the question. The question is open and precisely posed (R1).

One honest limit: an oracle that used Raman's own words as the two pillars and nothing
else scores 163 of 302 (54%), below the engine's 213 on the same rows. The verdict is not
made at the pillar; it is made in the weighing of house, lord, karaka, navamsa and yogas
together. Fixing the pillar is necessary, not sufficient.

Three patterns. (i) **Sub-matter mismatch**: the rule is about presence or means, the
golden row is about quality or outcome. (ii) **General dicta used as testimony**: Raman
states Kuja Dosha, malefics-in-Lagna and the like as background and then reasons past them;
the engine counts each firing as a vote. (iii) **Chart-wide geometric conditions** that are
satisfiable in a large share of all charts.

Against this, rule polarity as a whole is the best signal the engine has: benefic-only firing
→ Raman favourable 65 / afflicted 10 / mixed 9; malefic-only → afflicted 67 / favourable 13 /
mixed 11. The rules are right far more than they are wrong; a short list is doing the damage.

## 5. Cause D: coverage

On 21 rows no rule fired at all (Raman: favourable 11, afflicted 9, mixed 1); 6 of the 33
inversions are among them. In every one Raman names his reason in the cited passage. The
worst houses (H2, H1, H4, H5) are also the houses where Raman's reasons most often lean on a
specific configuration the rule set lacks (a dispositor, a maraka-from-karaka, a
Chandra-Lagna reading). Stated-position charts (all of Notable Horoscopes' older nativities)
compound this: no Shadbala, so every tie goes to mixed.

## 6. Cause E: a default that decides 50 rows with no information

The commonest single pattern in clause 2 on cast charts: both pillars strong, navamsa neutral,
lord not hard-afflicted. 50 rows. The engine returns favourable every time. Raman: favourable
27, afflicted 12, mixed 11. Because Cause B makes "both pillars strong" nearly universal, this
default is reached constantly, and it is reached by absence of evidence.

## 7. What this is not

It is not the late gates (9 misses in 61). It is not ayanamsa or casting (fixed 2026-10-23
and 2026-10-24; the Notable Horoscopes positions match the corrected book). It is not
something a threshold sweep will find: the ceiling analysis still stands for the judge as
designed, and the per-rule table above shows that the misses are concentrated in specific
rules and a specific missing concept, not spread as noise.

## 8. Recommendations, in order

Each one is a pre-registered measurement first and a code change only if the measurement
passes. None of them is tuning; each changes what the engine reads, with Raman's text as the
reason.

**R1. A Raman-grammar reading of lord and karaka condition, measured against his words
before it touches a verdict.** Build `lord_condition()` from the things Raman names:
uncancelled debility, combustion, malefic association or aspect not outweighed by a benefic
one, placement in 6/8/12 from the house, placement in the 12th from the matter. First gate:
agreement with Raman's own strong/weak words on the 254+269 rows above must beat Shadbala's
(currently 128/194 on the lord). Only then pre-register it as the pillar (variant V4, same
decision rule as the disposition prereg). Expected reach: Causes B and E, the largest surface.
Citations to carry: HTJAH-I:2338, :4283, :8434, HTJAH-II:7480 and the per-chart sentences
listed in the words dump.

**R2. Citation re-read of the ten contra-heavy rules.** For each, an engine-blind reviewer
reads the cited sentence and answers one question: does the sentence conclude about the
golden signification, or about a neighbour of it? Then re-scope (H11.C.2 → a `means_of_gain`
sub-matter; H5.C.1 → `birth_of_children`), demote general dicta to descriptive (H7.KD.1, which
Raman himself qualifies), or narrow chart-wide conditions (H2.C.69 to the Dwirdwadasa pairs
Raman actually counted in Charts 42 and 44). Expected reach: 30 to 50 rows touched; the per-
rule table is the before/after instrument.

**R3. Give the engine the two-sided answer Raman gives, and define it in the fixture.** In the
fixture, "mixed" should mean one thing: Raman's conclusion names both poles as operative (the
2-of-3 tiebreak wording already used for HTJAH-II h11_01). Rows that are weak-favourable or
weak-afflicted get re-labelled by an engine-blind reviewer; expect a handful. In the engine,
emit "mixed" only on positive evidence: decisive testimony on both sides (for instance a
fortifying yoga AND a hard affliction on the lord), and never as the stalemate value. Where
Raman's two sides are in sequence, the event-timing stage already owns the second half; the
verdict should say so instead of averaging. Expected reach: Cause A, up to 90 rows, the single
largest lever.

**R4. On stated-position charts, break ties with the testimony, not with a shrug.** When no
pillar exists, clause 2 should fall through to rule preponderance under the existing navamsa
and one-sided-rasi guards. Pre-register on the 43 stated-position clause-2 rows only; the
diagnostic that preponderance alone scores worse than the judge overall is about cast charts
and does not apply where there is nothing else to read.

**R5. Rule extraction from the 21 no-fire rows.** Each cites the passage where Raman gives the
reason. Encode the reason, cite it, measure its firing rate across the corpus before shipping
(the playbook's enumeration step). This also grows the rule set where H2, H1, H4 and H5 are
thin.

**R6. Seal the hold-out before any of R1 to R5 ships.** The 3HC example charts are the
candidate set. Without it, every gain above is in-sample, and this pool has been looked at
for a year.

## 9. What to expect, honestly

Within-one is already 91.7%: the engine is rarely far from Raman. The exact figure is held
down by shape (mixed) and by two pillars that do not see what Raman sees. R1 to R3 together
plausibly move exact into the high 70s on this pool, with the inversion count falling with
R1 and R2. Reaching 85% or more on exact would need R3 done well, because a third of the
misses are the engine answering a different question than "is it both?". None of this
touches the real-outcome axis, which stays NULL and is reported beside the fidelity figure,
never averaged with it.
